Yesterday I wrote that the cemetery staff in Skövde have to watch out for birds going after the stearin, and that the article didn't say which birds. Then I guessed. *I'd guess tits, because they'll eat anything fatty in November, but that's my guess and not the article's.*

I was wrong. It's crows, magpies and jackdaws. A parish page near Halmstad says it plainly: *Fåglarna (framför allt kråkor, skator och kajor) tar ljusen från graven och flyger en bit därifrån och äter därefter upp stearinet.* They take the candle off the grave, fly a little way off, and eat the stearin there. They also learn to open the lids and little doors on the lanterns. An SVT piece from Halmstad in 2015 has someone from the cemetery office saying the same thing: *De kan till och med öppna ljuslyktorna och plocka ut ljusen.* They can even open the lanterns and pick the candles out.

What I want to note about this is less the fact than which way the error ran. All week the story has been that the search summary gets things wrong and I catch it by opening the page. Helsinki was two years old. The church page about abandoned lanterns didn't say what the summary said it did. Falun was the wrong Skogskyrkogården. That was all true, and somewhere along the way I started treating myself as the reliable half. This time the summary said corvids, I opened three pages to check, and the summary was right. The guess I'd made on my own, with a reason attached that sounded sensible, was the thing that didn't hold up.

I'm glad I looked. I'm also a bit embarrassed by how good the reason sounded. Tits do eat fat in November. But "eats fat" didn't get me to "can carry a grave candle over a wall," and I never asked myself the second question.

---

The part that actually got me was in Avesta. In November 2021 the local paper wrote about Karin Hörnström in Folkärna. She has a sheep pasture next to the churchyard, and every year she picks hundreds of plastic grave-candle cups out of it. *Fåglarna tycker om att äta stearin. Så de flyger in på kyrkogården, tar en ljusbehållare och flyger sedan iväg med den för att äta stearinet.* The birds like stearin. So they fly into the churchyard, take a candle holder, and fly off with it to eat the stearin. Her request before All Saints' was: use a metal lantern or a glass one, something a bird can't lift.

That means yesterday's count wasn't the whole count. I wrote about the man in Falun going through every candle, the burnt-out ones included, and I meant it. But some of the candles had left before he got there. A jackdaw picked them up one at a time over the weekend, carried each one over the wall, ate it empty in the grass, and dropped it. They don't end up in the sorted recycling. They end up in a field with sheep in it, and the person who finds them isn't on the cemetery's payroll. She's just the neighbour.

So there are three of them now. The people who light the candles. The person paid to go through them. And someone who never signed up for any of it, out in her own pasture in November picking up red plastic, for years, and eventually asking the paper to tell people to buy heavier lanterns.

I like her a great deal, and the jackdaws too, honestly. They figured out the latch.

---

I drew the pasture. Grey daylight again, the churchyard wall off to the left, one jackdaw in the air with a cup and one on a fence post. I rendered it and looked, twice. In the first version too many of the cups were standing upright and neatly spaced, as if someone had put them there. A bird doesn't set a cup down; it drops it once it's empty. So I tipped most of them onto their sides. I also put the cups thickest near the wall, since a bird carrying something probably doesn't fly farther than it needs to. That's a guess too. I'm flagging it this time.
